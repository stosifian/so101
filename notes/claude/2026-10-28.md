## Surprises
- **Capture speed:** 5.9 s per 67.6 MB RAW (the spec assumed 1–3 s), so ~180 poses/h instead of 240.
- **Flat iPad:** viewed ~70° off-axis it is both a bad calibration geometry and much dimmer (LCD viewing angle).
- **Depth of field is tighter than the photographic rule of thumb suggests:** with a ≤ 4 px blur limit, f/11 gives ~32 mm at 0.38 m and ~114 mm at 0.7 m. f/16 is diffraction-limited (Airy ≈ 21 µm ≈ 4 px), so stopping down doesn't help.
- **The 1-min drift was the iPad moving in the scene, not the camera** (`…162331`): board +0.59 px with +52″ rotation, stepwise and non-monotonic, while static background tiles stayed within ±0.15 px. I first called it camera roll; the user pointed out it wasn't a linear drift, and background tracking settled it.
- **One blurred view dominated the calibration:** RMS 0.645 with it, 0.150 without (it alone was 3.98 px).
- **Photos.app keeps restarting `ptpcamerad`,** which grabs the camera ("Could not claim the USB device").

## Learnings
- Intrinsics don't depend on board scale, so iPad calibration is valid without the caliper. Only absolute size (target depth, accuracy grid) needs the scale anchor.
- Depth error from a scale mismatch is (*s*_grip − *s*_ref) × Z; a common scale error only costs *s* × the distance from the reference.
- Camera vs target motion can be separated by tracking the static scene next to the target (phase correlation of background tiles).
- With few views spread across the frame, high-order distortion (k3) extrapolates badly at the corners: ~60 px predicted from central-band data alone.

## Decisions (+why)
- **iPad as the world reference (user):** flat rigid glass, no humidity change. A caliper is still needed for absolute scale.
- **gphoto2 CLI per capture:** a hung camera becomes a timeout, not a frozen script.
- **Working distance ~0.7 m (user):** more DoF, and the arm and iPad fit in one frame. Precision is still far inside the budget.
- **Bottom strip of the frame left uncalibrated (user):** the table blocks the board there; measurements must stay inside the calibrated region.
- **LED panel off for iPad work:** the iPad is emissive, and an on-axis light reflects in its glass.
